Roof inspection

Photographs of every slope, the flashing details, and the attic side of the deck where we can get to it. Written up and handed over, whether or not you hire us for the work.

What we look at

A roof is an assembly, and most leaks are not in the field of shingle. They are at the joints. So the inspection works through the assembly rather than taking a glance at the surface.

  • Field of the roof. Granule condition, lifted or creased tabs, impact marks, blistering, and where any of it concentrates. Damage sitting over rafter lines means something different from damage scattered at random.
  • Edges. Eaves, rakes, drip edge, and whether the starter course was actually installed or improvised from cut shingle.
  • Penetrations. Pipe boots, which split with age and are the single most common leak we find. Vents, skylights, satellite mounts.
  • Flashing. Step and counter flashing at walls and chimneys, valleys, and anywhere a plane changes direction. This is where roofs leak.
  • Ventilation. Intake at the soffit and exhaust at the ridge, and whether they are balanced. Bad ventilation cooks shingles from underneath and causes blistering that gets mistaken for hail.
  • From the attic. Staining on the deck, daylight through the sheathing, wet insulation, rusted nail points. The underside tells you how long something has been happening.

What you get

A written report with the photographs in it, covering what we found, what is urgent, what can wait, and what is simply the roof being its age. If there is nothing wrong, the report says that.

If you are filing an insurance claim, the report is yours to hand to your adjuster. We do not make it conditional on booking work, and we do not keep it hostage.

Please do not go up there yourself

Walking an asphalt roof scuffs granules along your route and, in warm weather, presses dents into the soft asphalt. Inspectors call those scuffing and marring, neither counts as storm damage, and an adjuster who finds them has a reason to read the roof as damaged by something other than the weather. It is a genuinely common way for homeowners to wreck their own claim.

When to book one

  • After any storm that brought down branches in your neighbourhood.
  • When you can see shingle in the garden or the gutters.
  • When a stain appears on a ceiling, even a small one, and especially if it is not growing. Slow leaks do the most damage.
  • Before you buy the house.
  • Every couple of years once the roof passes ten, so you find out the roof is ending before the ceiling tells you.

What happens after

If the answer is a repair, we quote the repair. If the roof has reached the end, we say so and explain why, with the photographs to back it. Where it is genuinely borderline we will tell you that too, along with roughly how long we think you have.
