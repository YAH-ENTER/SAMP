Service 03  Stripped to the deck

Roof replacement

Everything comes off, the deck gets inspected and repaired where it needs it, and the assembly goes back on properly. We do not lay new shingles over old ones.

Why we tear off

A second layer laid over the first is faster and cheaper, and it is how a lot of roofs in this region got their current covering. It also means nobody looked at the deck, the new shingles sit on an uneven surface that telegraphs through, the assembly runs hotter, and the eventual tear-off costs more because there are two layers to take up.

More to the point, you cannot flash anything properly to a surface you have not exposed.

What the job involves

  • Tear off to the deck, with the ground protected and the landscaping covered.
  • Inspect the sheathing. Anything soft, delaminated or rotten comes out and gets replaced. This is where the number can move, so we tell you the per-sheet rate before we start rather than after we find it.
  • Drip edge at eaves and rakes.
  • Ice and water membrane at the eaves, in the valleys, and around penetrations.
  • Synthetic underlayment across the field.
  • Starter course at eaves and rakes, the manufacturer’s own, which is a condition of most higher wind warranties.
  • New flashing. Step, counter, valley, and new pipe boots. Reusing old flashing on a new roof is false economy and we do not do it.
  • Field shingles, nailed to the pattern the wind rating requires, which is often more nails than the minimum.
  • Ridge vent and ridge cap, with intake ventilation checked at the soffit so the system is balanced.
  • Clean up, including running a magnet over the grounds for nails.

Ask what the wind rating depends on

Manufacturer wind warranties are conditional. The higher tiers generally require a specific nail count per shingle and the manufacturer’s own starter strip at eaves and rakes, and the top tiers can require several matched accessories from the same brand. Miss one condition and the rating on the wrapper does not apply to your roof. Local code may also require more nails than the manufacturer’s minimum. Get the answer in writing, from us or from anyone else bidding.

How long, and what it is like

A typical house is one to three days depending on size, pitch and how much decking needs replacing. It is loud. Things will vibrate off walls, so take pictures down, and park away from the house.

If this is an insurance claim

Two things decide what you are paid, and both are in your policy rather than in our estimate.

  • The loss settlement section says whether you hold replacement cost or actual cash value cover. Under actual cash value, depreciation comes off and an older roof can pay out at a fraction of what the work costs.
  • The declarations page says whether a separate wind, hail or named-storm deductible applies, and whether it is a flat sum or a percentage of the dwelling limit. A percentage deductible on a large dwelling limit can exceed the cost of the repair.

Under replacement cost cover, most policies pay in two parts: the depreciated amount first, and the withheld depreciation once the work is actually completed. If the work never happens, the second payment never comes.

In Virginia there is a further trap. If you take an actual cash value payment and intend to claim the difference later, that claim has to be made within six months of the last such payment. It is a short window, separate from the policy’s own suit clause, and it is easy to miss.
