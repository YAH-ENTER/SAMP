01  Low slope & steep slope

Commercial
roofing systems

There is no best commercial roof. There is a roof that matches your slope, your rooftop traffic, what comes out of your exhaust fans, and how long you intend to own the building. Here is the whole market, and what each system is actually for.

02  Start here

Which roof suits
which purpose

Find the line that sounds like your building. The detail on each system is further down.

Warehouse or distribution

Large area, almost no rooftop traffic, cost per square foot matters most. Mechanically attached TPO or EPDM.

Restaurant or food processing

Grease and animal fats destroy most membranes. PVC or KEE, which are formulated to resist them.

Heavy rooftop equipment

Constant servicing means constant foot traffic. Modified bitumen or PVC with walkway pads laid to the units.

Cooling bill is the problem

A white reflective surface cuts heat gain. TPO or PVC, or a silicone coating over what is already there.

School, hospital, government

Long service life and redundancy outrank first cost. Built-up, modified bitumen, or standing seam metal.

A maze of penetrations

Curbs, pipes and odd shapes everywhere. Spray foam or a liquid-applied membrane, both of which go on without a seam.

Roof is sound but tired

Membrane still watertight, just weathered. A silicone or acrylic restoration coating buys ten to twenty years.

Holding the building for decades

Longest service life available, with the highest first cost. Standing seam metal, slate or clay tile.

Stormwater or urban heat rules

Jurisdiction requires retention or a cool roof. A vegetated assembly, or a ballasted system over a protected membrane.

04  Family one

Single-ply membranes

One sheet of engineered plastic or rubber, seamed into a continuous field. The most common low-slope roof in America, and the fastest to install.

TPO
Thermoplastic
polyolefin

A white plastic sheet, hot-air welded at the seams into one continuous membrane. It is the volume leader on American low-slope roofs because it is inexpensive, reflective, and quick to lay over large open fields.

  • SlopeLow slope, down to a quarter inch per foot
  • Life20 to 30 years, driven mostly by sheet thickness
  • StrengthsReflective, weldable seams, recyclable, good value
  • WeaknessesFormulations have changed repeatedly; grease attacks it
  • Best forWarehouses, retail boxes, schools, large simple roofs

PVC
Polyvinyl
chloride

The oldest thermoplastic single-ply still in wide use, and the one to reach for when something aggressive lands on the roof. Welded seams, same as TPO, with a long field record behind it.

  • SlopeLow slope
  • Life20 to 30 years
  • StrengthsResists grease, chemicals and fire; proven for decades
  • WeaknessesCosts more than TPO; older sheets could grow brittle
  • Best forRestaurants, food plants, labs, anything with a greasy exhaust

KEE
Ketone ethylene
ester

PVC with a solid plasticiser that cannot migrate out of the sheet, which is the failure mode that made old PVC go brittle. The most durable thermoplastic available, priced accordingly.

  • SlopeLow slope
  • Life30 to 40 years
  • StrengthsStays flexible; handles the worst chemical exposure
  • WeaknessesHighest single-ply cost; fewer crews trained on it
  • Best forIndustrial roofs where the exhaust is genuinely corrosive

EPDM
Synthetic
rubber

A rubber sheet, usually black, seamed with tape and adhesive rather than heat. It handles cold and thermal movement better than anything else in the single-ply family, which is why it has lasted fifty years in the market.

  • SlopeLow slope
  • Life20 to 30 years
  • StrengthsExcellent in cold; very large sheets mean few seams
  • WeaknessesBlack absorbs heat; taped seams are the weak point; oils attack it
  • Best forCold climates, simple roof shapes, tight budgets

05  Family two

Built-up and modified bitumen

Asphalt in layers. The oldest commercial roofing there is, and still the answer when you want redundancy, not one sheet standing between the building and the weather.

BUR
Built-up roofing
Tar and gravel

Alternating layers of asphalt and reinforcing felt, built up on the roof itself, then flooded with asphalt and covered in gravel. Three or four plies means three or four chances to stop water before it reaches the deck.

  • SlopeLow slope only
  • Life20 to 30 years
  • StrengthsRedundant plies; the gravel shields it from sun and hail
  • WeaknessesHeavy; slow to install; hot asphalt brings fumes and risk
  • Best forSchools, hospitals, anywhere redundancy is worth the weight

Mod-bit
APP and SBS
modified bitumen

Factory-made rolls of asphalt modified with polymer, so the sheet flexes instead of cracking. SBS is rubber-modified and stays flexible in cold. APP is plastic-modified and is torched down. Usually laid two plies thick with a granulated cap.

  • SlopeLow slope
  • Life20 to 30 years
  • StrengthsTough underfoot; higher tensile strength than most flat systems
  • WeaknessesTorch application is a fire risk on occupied buildings
  • Best forRoofs walked on constantly; small fields full of curbs

06  Family three

Metal

The longest service life you can buy, and the only commercial family that works well on steep slope and low slope alike. The distinction that matters is whether the fasteners are hidden or exposed.

Standing seam — concealed clips, panels free to move

  1. 01 Raised seam
  2. 02 Concealed clip
  3. 03 Underlayment
  4. 04 Insulation
  5. 05 Deck or purlins

Exposed fastener — screwed through the panel face

Every washer is a future leak

  1. 01 Ribbed panel
  2. 02 Screw + washer
  3. 03 Insulation
  4. 04 Purlins

Standing seam
Concealed
fastener

Panels lock together at a raised seam above the water line, held by clips that let the metal expand and contract. Nothing penetrates the weather surface, which is why these roofs outlive everything else on the list.

  • SlopeSteep slope, and low slope in structural profiles
  • Life40 to 60 years
  • StrengthsNo exposed fasteners; sheds snow; takes solar mounting well
  • WeaknessesHigh first cost; demands skilled installation; dents from hail
  • Best forLong holds, visible roofs, buildings you intend to keep

Exposed
fastener
R-panel

Corrugated or ribbed panels screwed straight through the face into purlins, with a rubber washer under every screw head. Cheap and fast. The washers are the service item, and they will need replacing before the panels do.

  • SlopeSteep slope
  • Life20 to 40 years, with washer replacement along the way
  • StrengthsLowest cost metal roof; quick to install and to patch
  • WeaknessesThousands of gasketed holes, each one a future leak
  • Best forAgricultural buildings, warehouses, outbuildings

Metal shingle
Stone-coated
steel

Steel pressed into the shape of shingle, shake or tile and finished with stone granules. It gives a steep-slope commercial building a traditional look with metal's weight and wind performance underneath.

  • SlopeSteep slope
  • Life40 to 50 years
  • StrengthsLight; strong wind ratings; hides minor hail denting
  • WeaknessesCosts more than shingle; repairs need matching stock
  • Best forMulti-family, hospitality, anywhere appearance is part of the brief

Metals
Copper, zinc,
aluminium

Beyond coated steel, the panel itself can be aluminium, which will not rust and suits coastal air; zinc, which patinas and self-heals fine scratches; or copper, which lasts a century and turns green doing it. All three are specified where the roof is part of the architecture.

  • AluminiumCorrosion-proof, light, good near salt air
  • Zinc60 to 100 years, develops a protective patina
  • Copper100 years plus, highest cost, unmistakable
  • Watch forGalvanic corrosion where dissimilar metals touch

07  Family four

Sprayed and liquid-applied

Systems that arrive in a drum and become a roof on site. Because there are no seams and no sheets, they go where rolled goods struggle: around dense penetrations, over odd shapes, and on top of roofs that are tired rather than finished.

Seamless, and it self-flashes every curb

  3. 03 Existing roof
  4. 04 Structural deck

Restoration coating over a sound existing membrane

  1. 01 Silicone top coat
  2. 02 Base coat
  3. 03 Existing membrane
  4. 04 Seams reinforced first
  5. 05 Structural deck

SPF
Spray
polyurethane foam

Two liquids meet at the spray gun, expand thirty times, and cure into rigid closed-cell foam that is both the insulation and the waterproofing. It is sprayed to whatever thickness is needed, which means it can be used to build slope into a dead-flat roof that ponds.

  • SlopeLow slope, and it can create slope where there is none
  • Life20 to 30 years, renewed by recoating every 10 to 15
  • StrengthsSeamless; self-flashes penetrations; high R per inch
  • WeaknessesWeather-sensitive install; needs a coating; birds peck bare foam
  • Best forRoofs crowded with curbs, and ponding roofs needing slope

Coatings
Silicone, acrylic,
polyurethane, PMMA

Rolled or sprayed over a roof that still holds water but has lost its surface. Seams are reinforced with fabric first, then the field is coated. It is a restoration, not a replacement, and it only works on a roof that is genuinely sound underneath.

  • SiliconeBest in ponding water; holds up to UV; attracts dirt
  • AcrylicCheapest, highly reflective, poor in standing water
  • PolyurethaneToughest underfoot, good where traffic is heavy
  • PMMACures fast even in cold; the usual choice for detail work
  • Not forWet insulation. Coating over trapped water buys nothing.

08  Family five

Specialty and steep slope

Vegetated assembly — extensive build-up

  1. 01 Planting + growing medium
  2. 02 Filter fabric
  3. 03 Drainage + retention layer
  4. 04 Insulation
  5. 05 Root-resistant membrane
  6. 06 Structural deck

Vegetated roofs

Extensive assemblies run three to six inches of medium with sedum and need almost no upkeep. Intensive assemblies are deeper, carry shrubs and people, and need a structure designed for the load. Both hold stormwater, cut the urban heat island, and shelter the waterproofing from the sun that normally kills it.

Ballasted and protected membrane

In a protected membrane roof the insulation goes above the waterproofing and river rock or concrete pavers hold it down. The membrane never sees sun, traffic or temperature swing. It is the standard build for plaza decks and roofs doubling as terraces.

Slate and tile

Natural slate runs 75 to 150 years and clay tile 50 to 100, which means the fasteners, flashings and deck will be replaced under a roof covering that outlives all of them. Both demand a structure rated for the weight. Usual on institutional, historic and ecclesiastical work.

Solar-integrated assemblies

Panels either get ballasted on racks or clamped to standing seam, which is the reason metal is specified on buildings planning to add solar later. Mounting anything to a single-ply roof means penetrations, and every one of them must be flashed as carefully as a pipe.

09  The brief

What actually
decides it

A survey answers these before anyone quotes a system. Specifying a membrane without them is guesswork.

01

Slope and drainage

Where does water actually go, and how long does it sit? Ponding rules out several systems on its own.

02

What is on the roof

Mechanical units mean traffic. Kitchen exhaust means grease. Both narrow the list immediately.

03

The deck

Steel, concrete, wood or gypsum changes attachment, fire rating and what the structure will carry.

04

How long you are holding it

A five-year hold and a thirty-year hold are different buildings as far as the roof is concerned.

05

Wind and code

Uplift requirements set the attachment pattern, and the pattern changes the price more than the membrane does.

06

Warranty

Material-only and NDL labour-and-material warranties are not comparable. Read which one you are being quoted.
