What we actually do

Residential and commercial roofing. Inspections, repairs, replacement, new installation, and the full range of low-slope commercial systems. We also do the gutter, fascia and carpentry work that tends to come with a roof, because leaving that to somebody else is how detail gets missed at the joints.

Most of our work arrives one of three ways: a leak somebody wants traced, a roof that has reached the end and needs replacing, or a storm that has just gone through and left a homeowner trying to work out whether they have a claim.

How we quote

We go up and look first. Every slope gets photographed, the flashing details get photographed, and where there is attic access we look at the deck from underneath, because the underside tells you things the surface will not.

Then you get a written report with the photographs in it. If you are filing an insurance claim, that report is yours to hand to your adjuster. If you decide to use somebody else, it is still yours. We do not hold it back.

Where a repair will do, we say so. There is more money in a replacement, and we would rather have the next job and the one after that.

Licence and credentials

We hold a Virginia Class A Contractor licence, number 2705175716. Class A is the tier with no dollar limit on the value of a single contract or on total annual work, and it carries the highest experience and financial requirements of Virginia’s three classes.

Worth asking any roofer, including us

  • What is your licence number, and which class?
  • Who carries general liability, and who carries workers’ compensation on the crew that will be on my roof?
  • Is the warranty labour and material, or material only?
  • Is the crew employed by you or subcontracted?

A contractor who will not answer those in writing is telling you something.

What we will not do

We do not knock on doors after storms. We do not offer to cover your deductible, which is prohibited conduct for a contractor in Virginia and a fraudulent insurance act in Maryland when it is done to defraud the carrier. The Maryland Insurance Administration names “free roof” offers outright as part of a fraud scheme in which ordinary wear and tear gets presented to an insurer as storm damage.

If somebody has told you your roof is totalled before an adjuster has been up there, be careful. Read our damage articles and you will be in a better position to judge it yourself.
