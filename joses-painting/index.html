What we paint

The six jobs we get called for most, and a longer list under them.

Interior painting

Walls, ceilings, trim, doors, closets, and stairwells. We move what we can and cover what we can’t, fill the nail holes, caulk the gaps, and roll two coats. Furniture goes back where we found it.

  • Bedrooms, living rooms, kitchens, baths
  • Two-story stairwells and high ceilings
  • Basements and garages

Exterior painting

Siding, trim, soffits, fascia, shutters, porches, and front doors. We wash it first, scrape and sand whatever is failing, prime bare wood and stains, then coat it. Vinyl, wood, fiber cement, brick, and stucco.

  • Full repaints and trim-only refreshes
  • Peeling and chalking siding
  • Garage doors and railings

Kitchen cabinet painting

Doors and drawers come off and get labeled so they go back on the right box. We degrease, sand, prime, and spray a hard finish that takes hands and steam. Cheaper than new boxes by a wide margin.

  • Kitchens, islands, and vanities
  • Oak grain filled smooth on request
  • New hinges and pulls installed

Drywall repair and installation

Nail pops, cracked corner bead, doorknob holes, ceiling water damage, and full sheets in a new basement. We hang, tape, mud, sand, and match the texture that’s already there before anything gets painted.

  • Patches from a quarter to a full wall
  • Knockdown and orange peel texture matching
  • Popcorn ceiling removal and resurfacing

Deck cleaning, staining and sealing

We strip off the gray, the mildew, and the old flaking finish, let the boards dry all the way, then brush or spray stain and sealer. Fences, pergolas, and porch rails get the same treatment.

  • Wood cleaning and brightening
  • Semi-transparent and solid stain
  • Railings, stairs, and lattice

Pressure washing

Siding, driveways, walkways, patios, and fences. Anything that shouldn’t take pressure gets a soft wash instead, which is detergent and a low-pressure rinse rather than a tip that drives water behind the siding.

  • House washing before a repaint
  • Concrete and paver cleaning
  • Mildew and algae removal

How a job goes

Four steps, and you know the price before anybody opens a can.

  1. 1

    You call or send the form

    Tell us the rooms or the surfaces. Photos help a lot. We ask a few questions and set a time to come look.

  2. 2

    We come out and measure

    We walk the job with you, check what shape the surfaces are in, and talk through colors and sheen. You get the price in writing, itemized, with what is and isn’t in it.

  3. 3

    We prep

    Furniture moved or covered, floors protected, edges taped, holes patched, glossy trim sanded, stains primed. Prep is most of the work, and it decides how the finish looks in three years.

  4. 4

    We paint, then clean up

    Two coats unless the estimate says otherwise. We clean up at the end of every day and haul our own debris. Last stop is a walkthrough with you, list in hand, before you pay the balance.

What homeowners ask

How much does painting cost in Woodbridge?

It depends on square footage, ceiling height, how many colors you want, and how much patching the walls need. One bedroom is a different job from an open two-story stairwell. You get a written price after we see it, and looking is free.

How long will my house be torn up?

Two or three interior rooms usually run one to three days. A whole-house interior is closer to a week. Exteriors depend on weather. We give you the window before we start and call you if it moves.

Do you supply the paint?

Yes, and the estimate includes it. If you want a specific brand or product line, tell us and we price it that way.

Can you match a color I already have?

Usually. Bring us an old can or let us take a chip off the wall and the paint store can scan it. We can also match from a fan deck on site if you would rather not cut the wall.

Can you paint while we are living here?

Most of our work is in occupied homes. We use low-odor paint where it matters, keep a room usable, and plan around kids and pets if you tell us what is where.

Why does deck staining take more than one day?

Cleaning is its own step. We clean the boards, then they have to dry, usually about two days depending on humidity. Stain over damp wood is how a finish peels inside a year.
